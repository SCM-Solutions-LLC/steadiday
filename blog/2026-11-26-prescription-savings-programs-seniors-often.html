Prescription Savings Programs Seniors Often Overlook

November 26, 2026 • By SteadiDay Team • 6 min read

Prescription costs rarely show up as one big bill. They arrive as a $45 copay here, a skipped refill there, and a pharmacy receipt you fold into your wallet without reading. Several prescription savings programs seniors qualify for go unused, and most take less than an hour to check. This post follows one day, morning to night, and shows where each program fits into things you already do.

Morning: Look at What's on the Counter

Start with your pill organizer or the bottles by the coffee maker. Write down each medication's name, strength, and how many you take per day. Note whether it's a generic or a brand name (the label or your pharmacy receipt will say). This list is the tool you'll use for every step below, because every savings program starts with the exact drug and dose.

Add two more details to the list: what you paid at the last fill and which pharmacy you used. Prices for the same generic can differ by pharmacy, and you can't spot a bargain without a baseline.

Mid-Morning: Check Medicare Extra Help

Extra Help, also called the Part D Low-Income Subsidy, lowers premiums, deductibles, and copays for Medicare drug coverage. According to the National Council on Aging's eligibility chart, the program can save older adults thousands of dollars a year, and people with incomes up to 150% of the federal poverty level qualify. Resource limits apply too, though your home and one vehicle generally don't count.

Many people who qualify never apply. SIRUM estimates that 2 million or more eligible seniors haven't enrolled. Some assume their income is too high without checking, and some don't know the program exists.

You can apply through the Social Security Administration at ssa.gov/extrahelp or by calling 1-800-772-1213. Have your income and savings information nearby. If your state Medicaid office or Medicare Savings Program already covers you, you may be enrolled automatically, so check your mail for a notice before reapplying.

Late Morning: Call Your State Health Insurance Program

Every state has a State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP), staffed by trained counselors who explain Medicare for free. Search "SHIP" plus your state name, or call the Medicare line at 1-800-633-4227 and ask for your local office. A counselor can screen you for Extra Help, review whether your Part D plan covers your specific drugs, and point you to state pharmaceutical assistance programs if your state has one. Bring the medication list you made earlier. The call usually works best when you can read off drug names and doses.

Midday: Price Your Generics Outside the Usual Pharmacy

Lunchtime is a good moment to run a few price checks online. Mark Cuban Cost Plus Drug Company sells many generics at the manufacturer's cost plus a fixed markup and a pharmacy fee. A published analysis in PharmacoEconomics estimated that Medicare Part D would have saved about $8.6 billion if beneficiaries had used Cost Plus pricing for 90-day generic supplies. That figure describes the whole program, not your personal bill, but it shows how wide the price gaps can be.

Compare three numbers for each generic: your Part D copay, the Cost Plus price, and the cash price using a free discount card such as GoodRx or SingleCare. Sometimes the cash price beats your insurance copay. Keep in mind that prescriptions paid for in cash usually don't count toward your Part D deductible or out-of-pocket cap, so ask your pharmacist whether that tradeoff matters for your situation. If you're on several expensive brand-name drugs, insurance will likely come out ahead. If you take mostly inexpensive generics, cash may win.

Afternoon: Ask About 90-Day Supplies and Manufacturer Help

Midafternoon is when most pharmacies are quieter, so it's a good time to call or stop in. Ask these questions:

  • Is a 90-day supply cheaper per pill than a 30-day supply on my plan?
  • Does mail-order cost less than the retail counter?
  • Is there a lower-cost generic or therapeutic alternative for any drug on my list?

For brand-name drugs without a generic, check the manufacturer's patient assistance program. Many drugmakers run foundations that provide medication free or at low cost to people who meet income rules, and Medicare enrollees are often eligible. Your prescriber's office usually has the application forms and can fill in the medical section. Nonprofit lists such as NeedyMeds can help you find the right program by drug name.

Evening: Compare Plans When Open Enrollment Arrives

Part D plans change their drug lists (formularies) and prices every year. Medicare's open enrollment runs October 15 to December 7, and the Plan Finder tool at medicare.gov lets you enter your exact medications and pharmacy to compare total yearly costs. An evening at the computer with your list is enough to run the comparison. If you'd rather not do it alone, a SHIP counselor will do it with you.

If you're using a phone or tablet for the search and you live alone, it helps to have a safety net while you're up and moving around the house. SteadiDay's Fall Detection feature is included on the free plan and can alert someone if you take a fall.

For related reading on managing what you take, see our posts on osteoporosis drugs and dementia risk and vitamin B12 deficiency after 50, both of which are good prompts for a medication review with your doctor.

Before Bed: Set Up Next Month's Savings

Finish the day with a short note on the same page as your medication list: which program you'll apply for, which prices you checked, and which question you'll ask at your next appointment. Put one refill reminder on your calendar so you aren't tempted to ration doses when money is tight. Skipping or splitting pills to stretch a supply can cause real harm, and your doctor or pharmacist can usually find a cheaper option once they know cost is the barrier.

Common Questions

Who qualifies for Medicare Extra Help?

People with Medicare Part D and incomes up to 150% of the federal poverty level generally qualify, as long as their savings and other resources fall under the program's limits. Your home and one vehicle usually don't count toward those limits. You can apply through the Social Security Administration online or by phone.

Is Mark Cuban's Cost Plus Drug Company cheaper than insurance?

It can be for many generics, but not always. Compare the Cost Plus price against your insurance copay and a discount card price for each drug. Cash purchases often don't count toward your Part D deductible, so factor that in if you take costly brand-name medications.

Can I use a discount card if I have Medicare Part D?

Yes, you can choose to pay cash with a discount card instead of using your insurance for a particular prescription. The purchase typically won't count toward your plan's out-of-pocket spending. Ask your pharmacist to run both prices before you decide.

Where can I get free help comparing drug coverage?

Your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) offers free, unbiased Medicare counseling. Call 1-800-633-4227 to be directed to your local office. Bring a list of your medications and doses so the counselor can compare plans accurately.

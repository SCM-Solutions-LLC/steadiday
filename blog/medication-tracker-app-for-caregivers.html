Medication Tracker Apps for Caregivers: What to Look For (2026)

How to choose a medication app that lets you see whether a parent took their medicines, get alerted when it matters, and help from a distance.

Last updated: November 12, 2026

If you help a parent or spouse with their medicines, you mostly need answers to two questions: did they take today's doses, and will you find out if something goes wrong? Most medication apps were built for the person taking the pills. Only a few are built for the person checking in from across town or across the country.

This guide covers what a caregiver should look for in a medication tracker app, compares how four apps handle the caregiver side, and walks through setting one up for someone who isn't comfortable with technology. SteadiDay publishes this guide, so we've been specific about what our own app does and doesn't do.

What Caregivers Need From a Medication App

Before comparing apps, decide which of these matter for your situation. Families rarely need all of them.

A record you can see without asking. The app should log each dose as taken or skipped, and you should be able to see that record from your own phone. That way you aren't calling every evening to ask "did you take your pills?"

An alert when a dose is missed. Some apps notify a family member when a reminder goes unanswered. If missed doses could be dangerous, as with blood thinners, insulin, seizure medicines or heart rhythm drugs, this is the feature to prioritize.

Who controls the schedule. Some apps let a caregiver add or change medicines remotely. Others keep the schedule on the parent's phone, so changes happen there. Remote editing is convenient when you manage everything. Local control suits a parent who is still running their own routine and just wants someone to keep an eye out.

How much the caregiver has to set up. Some apps need the caregiver to install the same app and create an account. Others send a link you open in a browser. The fewer accounts involved, the more likely the arrangement survives a new phone or a forgotten password.

A design the person taking the medicines will actually use. A detailed record only helps if your parent taps the reminder. Large text, few screens and a reminder that's hard to dismiss by accident matter more than any caregiver dashboard.

Something beyond pills. For many families the bigger worry is a fall or an emergency, not a missed vitamin. If that's you, look for an app that also alerts you to those.

SteadiDay

SteadiDay is an iPhone app for adults 50+ that pairs medication reminders with fall detection and an SOS button. Your parent scans a medication label or types the name and picks the times. Every dose they check off is recorded. The free plan covers up to 5 medications.

The caregiver feature is Share Activity, part of Premium. Your parent can share a private Care Summary with you: the week's medication streak, today's medicines and reminders, their daily check-in and step count. It's a web link, so you don't install anything or create an account, and your parent can switch it off whenever they like.

It has limits. SteadiDay does not text you when a dose is missed. You see missed doses when you open the Care Summary. You also can't change the medication schedule from your phone; that happens on your parent's iPhone. The automatic texts SteadiDay does send are for emergencies: if a hard fall is detected and your parent doesn't respond within 30 seconds, or they press SOS, trusted contacts get a text with their location. That part is free and needs a free account.

Medisafe

Medisafe is one of the longest-running medication apps and the most direct fit if missed-dose alerts are your priority. Its Medfriend feature gives a family member a view-only connection to the medication schedule. If a reminder goes unanswered, the Medfriend gets a notification about 30 minutes after the final reminder, usually around an hour after the scheduled time. Medisafe moved to paid subscriptions in early 2026, and the free tier is now limited in some regions, so check what's included where you live.

Caring Village

Caring Village is a family caregiving app more than a pill reminder. Alongside medication reminders and adherence tracking, it has a shared care calendar, to-do tasks family members can volunteer for, and a journal for updates. It suits families splitting care among several siblings. The download is free, and its paid plans start at $14.99/month.

MyTherapy

MyTherapy is a free, ad-free medication reminder and health diary that lets you involve a family member. We couldn't confirm from its current listing whether a family member gets alerted to a missed dose, so test that yourself before relying on it.

Which App Fits Your Situation

If a missed dose could be serious and you need to know within the hour, choose an app with missed-dose alerts to family. Of the apps here, Medisafe is the clearest about how its alert works.

If several family members share the caregiving, Caring Village's shared calendar and task list do more than any medication app.

If your bigger worry is a fall or an emergency, and you want to glance at medications rather than be alerted about each one, SteadiDay covers both in one app with a simple home screen. Your parent stays in control of their own schedule, and you check a private link when you want to.

Setting It Up for a Parent

Whichever app you choose, setup goes better if you do it together, in person or on a video call, rather than handing over a phone with an app already installed.

1. Gather the medication list first. Collect every bottle, including vitamins and over-the-counter medicines, or print the list from the pharmacy. Ask the pharmacist to confirm times and anything that shouldn't be taken together.

2. Match reminders to existing habits. Set reminder times to things that already happen every day, like breakfast or the evening news. A reminder tied to a routine gets tapped. One at an arbitrary time gets dismissed.

3. Agree on what you'll see. Tell your parent exactly what the caregiver view shows and when you'll look at it. People are more willing to share when they know it's a safety net, not surveillance.

4. Test the caregiver side. Have your parent skip one reminder on purpose, then check that it shows up on your end the way you expect.

5. Check in after the first week. Look for patterns, like missed evening doses, and adjust times rather than adding more reminders.

When an App Isn't Enough

An app reminds and records. It can't make sure the right pill comes out of the right bottle. If your parent is mixing up medicines, doubling doses or forgetting whether they took something minutes ago, add a physical system.

Pharmacy adherence packaging. Many pharmacies will pre-sort medicines into sealed packets or blister cards labeled by date and time. Ask whether yours offers it and whether there's a fee.

A weekly pill organizer. Filling a 7-day, multi-compartment organizer once a week, ideally by you or another family member, makes a missed dose visible at a glance.

Automated dispensers. Locked dispensers that release only the current dose can help with memory problems, but they cost more and someone still has to load them correctly.

A medication review. Ask the doctor or pharmacist to review the full list once a year, or after any hospital stay. Fewer medicines, or fewer doses a day, can do more for adherence than any reminder.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best medication tracker app for caregivers?

It depends on what you need to know. If you need an alert when a dose is missed, Medisafe's Medfriend feature is built for that. If several family members share caregiving, Caring Village adds a shared calendar and tasks. If you also want fall detection and SOS alerts, SteadiDay combines those with medication reminders and a private Care Summary for family.

Is there a medication app that notifies family when a dose is missed?

Yes. Medisafe notifies a Medfriend about 30 minutes after the final reminder for a dose goes unanswered. SteadiDay does not send missed-dose alerts; family see missed doses in the Care Summary, and SteadiDay's automatic texts are reserved for detected falls and SOS.

Can I track my parent's medications from my own phone?

Yes, with most caregiver-focused apps. Medisafe and Caring Village connect through their apps. SteadiDay's Share Activity (Premium) uses a private web link, so you don't need to install anything or create an account.

Can a caregiver change a parent's medication schedule remotely?

Not in Medisafe, where the Medfriend role is view-only, or in SteadiDay, where the schedule stays on your parent's iPhone. If remote editing matters to you, confirm it with the app before you commit.

Is CareZone still available?

No. Walmart bought CareZone's technology in 2020, and the app was retired in early 2023. Medisafe, Caring Village and MyTherapy are common replacements for its family features.

Is there a free medication tracker for caregivers?

MyTherapy is free. SteadiDay's medication reminders, fall detection and SOS are free, but the caregiver Care Summary needs Premium. Medisafe and Caring Village have free downloads with paid plans for some features, and those plans vary by region.
